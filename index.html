01 · The millennium challenge

Why a fluid must not be allowed to tear.

For more than two centuries the mathematical physics community has lived with an incomplete sentence about the Navier–Stokes equations. They describe the air over a wing and the swirl in a cup. They do not yet, in three dimensions, confess whether they remain smooth for all time.

The fear has a name: blow-up. Vorticity intensifies at a point until velocity becomes infinite in finite time, and the model ceases to represent the world. The Clay Millennium Prize asked the question as a detective story. Does every smooth, divergence-free initial field stay smooth forever — or can a perfect fluid spontaneously become a singularity?

The analytic school tried to fight the nonlinearity with estimates. In 3D the vortex-stretching term outruns the bounds. The geometric school placed the fluid on Arnold’s coadjoint orbits, then stopped at ideal flow. Viscosity and stretching were left outside the picture.

Frohmanian Symplectohydrodynamics begins where those two paths fail to meet: a geometric completion in which the equations are given the missing 2-form demanded by their own stretching, and thereby refuse their own destruction.

The incomplete sentence

Does every smooth, divergence-free initial velocity field on the periodic torus yield a globally smooth solution for all time? That is Fefferman’s statement (B). It is still open.

The Frohmanian program does not put an estimate on top of chaos and call it a proof. It separates a kernel-closed geometric tether from one named analytic estimate. Beale–Kato–Majda continues a solution when the vorticity integral is finite. It does not prove that the integral is finite.

Clay status · open · reduction, not a certificate

02 · Two paths to truth

Estimates fight. Geometry completes.

The geometric layer · kernel-closed

Give the fluid its missing rails.

Arnold and Khesin placed ideal flow on coadjoint orbits. The Frohmanian tether is the minimal correction forced by (C1)–(C3): locality, strict degeneracy through \(\Pi_u\), and negative feedback at vorticity maxima.

What that does not closeThe tether does not rewrite the Navier–Stokes equation, and it does not derive the cubic rate. Geometry is proved. The depletion bound is not.

04 · Two layers, not a circle

Geometry first. The rate stays named.

The argument is two layers, and the second is not hidden inside the first. Layer 1 is the symplectic tether on coadjoint orbits. It is kernel-closed. Layer 2 is a tethered Lyapunov inequality. Its cubic damping is a named estimate, not a conclusion smuggled back in as geometry. The KKS pairing and the metric kernel \(B_F\) are different objects. \(\Pi_u\) is the projector orthogonal to the kinetic-energy Casimir.

$$\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{KKS}}=\int\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\Big(\frac{\delta F}{\delta\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times\frac{\delta G}{\delta\boldsymbol{\omega}}\Big)\,dV$$ $$B_F(F,G)=-\kappa\int|\boldsymbol{\omega}|^2\Big(\Pi_u\frac{\delta F}{\delta\boldsymbol{\omega}}\cdot\Pi_u\frac{\delta G}{\delta\boldsymbol{\omega}}\Big)\,dV,\qquad \kappa=C_{\mathrm{CZ}}(3)=\tfrac{3}{2}$$
Law I · proved

Geometric tether

(C1) locality and invariance. (C2) strict degeneracy through \(\Pi_u\). (C3) negative feedback at maxima of vorticity. KKS skew-symmetry, the cyclic Jacobi identity, idempotence and self-adjointness of \(\Pi_u\), Casimir conservation, and non-degeneracy of the kernel on the Casimir defect are unconditional. So is uniqueness of the minimal tether, antisymmetric and symmetric.

Law II · proved from the rate

Comparison ceiling

If the vorticity maximum obeys \(M'\le C M^2-\kappa'' M^3\), then \(M(t)\le\max(M(0), C/\kappa'')\). That is comparison_ode_ceiling. It is an ODE theorem. It does not derive the inequality from Navier–Stokes.

Law III · reduction

Not a solution

clay_reduces_to_direction_curvature_bound: for every \(\nu>0\), the classical periodic Beale–Kato–Majda continuation principle together with DirectionCurvatureBound implies Fefferman’s exact periodic statement. BKM is a criterion, not a proof that the vorticity stays bounded. The cubic depletion bound is the single open estimate. Clay status: open. The converse is not proved.

05 · The scaffold

Five beams. One of them is open.

Read this top to bottom. A later beam is not allowed to justify an earlier one. The retired comparison \(M'\le C-M^2\) is not the comparison in the corpus. The comparison that is proved is cubic.

$$y'\le C y^2-\kappa'' y^3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y(t)\le\max\big(y(0),\, C/\kappa''\big)$$
01 · closed

Geometric layer

Coadjoint orbit, (C1)–(C3), KKS bracket, Jacobi identity, \(\Pi_u\), Casimir non-degeneracy, uniqueness of the minimal kernel. Modules: SymplecticTether, SymplecticConstraint. Introduced before any regularity claim. Zero sorry.

02 · open

Direction-curvature bound

The single named estimate: \(M'\le C M^2-\kappa M^3\), high-set depletion of stretching by curvature of the vorticity direction. ns_global_regularity_of_cubic_damped_rate assumes this rate and does not derive it. global_regularity_for_NS assumes a vorticity ceiling and does not derive it. Calderón–Zygmund fails at \(p=\infty\); a pointwise bound \(\|\nabla u\|_\infty\le C\|\omega\|_\infty\) is false in general and is not a theorem here.

03 · closed

Comparison ceiling

Any differentiable solution of the cubic differential inequality stays under \(\max(y(0), C/\kappa'')\). This is ODE comparison. It becomes a fluid theorem only when beam 02 is proved from the equation.

04 · criterion

Beale–Kato–Majda

If \(\int_0^T\|\omega\|_\infty\,dt\) is finite, a smooth solution continues. A uniform ceiling would make that integral finite. The criterion does not produce the ceiling. In the formalization it is the explicit hypothesis PeriodicBKMContinuation (Kato 1972 / Beale–Kato–Majda 1984), cited and not re-proved.

05 · closed

The reduction

clay_reduces_to_direction_curvature_bound. For every \(\nu>0\), beam 02 together with beam 04 implies Clay_B_exact, Fefferman’s periodic statement. The Lean domain EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3) is not that statement; the named reduction is. The converse is not proved. Axioms reported by the kernel: propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. No nonstandard axiom. Clay status: open.

Holographic

A conjectural diagnostic, not a step in the reduction. Boundary turbulence read against a bulk horizon. It is not cited as a proof of statement (B).

A dual is a picture. It is not DirectionCurvatureBound.

08 · Application

Tether the plasma, not the estimate.

Reactor control, in this architecture, is a proposed use of the same geometry — not a second proof of Navier–Stokes. Stretching would have to be suppressed without rewriting conservation laws. The tether is the control proposal. The cubic rate remains open.

Metriplectic extension restores viscosity and entropy so the plasma can relax into high-beta equilibria. A tethered Lyapunov functional watches enstrophy precursors. RF injection and edge micro-actuators become synthetic metric corrections — transport barriers on demand.

The holographic dual is a diagnostic: boundary turbulence as a window onto bulk horizon shifts, disruptions visible before they arrive as surface instabilities.

A parallel research thread asks how Boolean and other discrete logical structure can be preserved — not smeared away — when it is computed into numerical integrators and continuous functionals of plasma MHD and FSymHD.

Operational chain, same scaffold

1. Local existence — Kato, classical.

2. Cubic rate — open. \(M'\le C M^2-\kappa'' M^3\). Not the retired quadratic comparison, and not \(\kappa=1\).

3. BKM — a criterion. A ceiling would make the vorticity integral finite. The criterion does not give the ceiling.

4. Reduction — Clay (B) follows from the rate and classical continuation. It is not a certificate of global smooth operation.
